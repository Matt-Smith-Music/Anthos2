What this does, and what it can't

Read this once — it will save you arguing with it.

Changes listens to the whole track, works out how much energy sits on each of the twelve pitch classes in every 93 ms slice, and matches that shape against a set of chord templates. It then picks the most likely run of chords across the track, so a chord has to earn its place for a moment before it is allowed to change.

It is not transcribing the notes. It never decides that a G was played at 0:14 — it only decides that the harmony around 0:14 looks like a G. That distinction is why it copes with a full band but will never give you the riff.

Where it is strong: clear pop, rock, folk and soul with a steady rhythm section. Where it struggles, and you should trust your ears over it:

  • heavy distortion — the overtones smear the chroma
  • dense mixes, big pads, orchestration
  • relative majors and minors (C vs Am share two notes out of three)
  • inversions and pedal notes
  • fast changes — anything under about a third of a second is invisible to it
  • extensions: a 9th chord usually comes out as the plain 7th, and an added 9th as the plain triad

The one to reach for first is Chord detail in settings. A singer holding a note over a chord looks exactly like part of that chord to this kind of analysis, which is what makes chords come out fancier than they are. Turning detail down makes an extension earn its place against the plain triad. Turn it up for jazz or soul, where the 7ths are really there.

Trust the bass decides how much the lowest instrument gets to name the root. Turn it up where the bass player is plainly stating the root and the chords are coming out as the wrong one of two that share most of their notes, like E minor instead of G. Turn it down on tracks with a busy or walking bass line.

Chord changes sets how quick it is to call a change. Past "keen" it also shortens the window it listens through, from about a third of a second to under a fifth, which is what lets it catch a chord that only lasts half a beat. The cost is that low notes need a long look to tell apart, so the bass gets read less accurately up there. If changes are being missed, push it up; if the same chord starts breaking into pieces, pull it back.

Chords it is unsure about are dimmed with an amber dot. Those are the ones to check first. Fix chords lets you correct anything it got wrong, and the chart you copy out is the corrected one.

The track is read straight off your device. Nothing is sent anywhere, and it works with no internet.
