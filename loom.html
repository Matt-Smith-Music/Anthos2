How Loom works

Drop audio loops onto the timeline (or tap + Audio). Loom works out each loop's tempo from its length and its hits, then plays it at the project tempo.

Modes

Slice cuts the loop at its hits and re-places each hit on the grid. Drums keep their punch at any tempo. Slowing down leaves gaps between hits; if that bothers you, use Stretch.

Stretch changes the speed without changing the pitch. Hits are anchored so they stay sharp. Best for bass, guitar, keys and vocals.

Repitch speeds up or slows down like a tape or a sampler, so the pitch moves with the tempo.

One-shot plays the file once, unchanged, from where the clip starts.

Quantise

Pick a grid and every hit moves to its nearest grid line. Strength sets how far it moves (50% tightens without killing the feel). Hits sets how many transients Loom finds; tap a marker to remove a wrong one, tap empty waveform to add one.

Loop tempo

If the detected tempo is double or half what you expect, use ÷2 or ×2, or set the number of bars. Put the tempo in the file name (for example groove 96bpm.wav) and Loom will use it.

Timeline

Drag a clip to move it (up or down to change track); drag its right edge to lengthen it and the loop repeats. Tap the ruler to move the playhead. Drag the ends of the loop brace on the ruler to set the loop. Space plays and pauses on a keyboard.
